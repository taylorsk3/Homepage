A Handmade Web

The article A Hand Made Web was a very impact read. Things crafted from scratch are really making a comeback with today's dead internet theory. There's a lot of value that comes with something that's handcrafted and this article really explained that well about online based platforms. When I think of things handmade I think of physical, tangible things that you can hold, but this article made me realize that software can be handmade, without being a physical article. There are so many tools and so many pre-made softwares that already exist that you can build from, rather than coding every word and every line from scratch. I remember the web looking like organic handmade code in my early early age, and it's very cool to realize now in my older age why it looks like that. I'm not much of a technology person but understanding the basics of code really help me understand how people interact with online platforms, which is a great skill to have as a graphic designer. A lot of my work is physical, but this made me more confident to do more online, or digital work.

The Creative Independent

What is a website? A bundle of files on an online portfolio. A handmade web, like html and javascript, but these don't tend to exist in the 2026 world. So how would one go about coding for a huge creative website? Can you do that with a simple script, like how we learned in class?

“A website can be anything. It doesn’t (and probably shouldn’t) be an archive of your complete works. That’s going to be dead the moment you publish. A website, or anything interactive, is inherently unfinished. It’s imperfect—maybe sometimes it even has a few bugs. But that’s the beauty of it.” Very interesting take. My current website is hosted through a third party coding program, meaning it is already coded, and I pay Cargo to do the heavy lifting. My website is just an archive of my current graphic design work. How can I expand this idea? What can I add to make it a growing location of ideas and information, a place where others want to come back too. A location for others,not just me.

This website was great,relating the internet, or the online web to organic nature. I can always appreciate this concept, because everything comes from agriculture, and it is always fun to think about how something evolved from what aden and eve saw when they were on the same earth we are on.You don't associate the online web with agriculture, but they are directly related.

A Rant About Technology

Everything is technology, or at least how we interact with the world is considered technology. Ursula K. Le Guin put it nicely in her paper, a rant about Technology: “Technology is the active human interface with the material world.” I remember learning this concept in high school history class. It seems like an obvious concept, but with the amount of big tech that consumes every task, it has overthrown the word and the meaning. Technology , when you strip it to its simplest form can be a lever, or a pulley, some sort of system that is an addition to our human body's capability.



Against an Increasingly User-Hostile Web

This reading reviewed some behaviors as humans navigate across the web. This essay was written almost 10 years ago, and it is just as prevalent now than ever. There seems to be an attention grab, not necessarily a hook, but an urgency of one to interact with the web. Users no longer become for the user, but for profit. “Sign Up Here!” ‘Join Our Email List Here!” “Like, Comment, Subscribe, follow on instagram and twitter, and make sure to hit that notification bell, also comment your favorite fruit!” It seems to be getting gout of hand and harder to ignore. The article goes on about how the user is just a point of data, we trained waymo- for free- by doing those “make sure you're not a robot” check all the boxes that are bikes. WE TRAINED WAYMO…

Keeping us addicted to our phones creates a sense of urgency, loyalty, and connection. These websites are addictive, this is nothing new. The modern web has borrowed a lot of its tendencies from gambaling… These tactics make us dependent on technology, which creates a cycle of us coming back, which drives profits.

This dependencies remind me of the new iphone drop that just happened. There is a fad, but also a real movement of people reverting back to physical media and more away from technology, slowly as the dead internet theory proves itself. To me this phone seemed almost too deaf to what the people wanted.
